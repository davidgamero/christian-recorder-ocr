A clearer window into the archive

The Christian Recorder, published by the African Methodist Episcopal Church, machine-transcribed for search. Penn’s serial catalog led us to 44 volumes at Internet Archive. This is an independent research edition.

What the numbers mean

We compare GLM output with Archive’s existing OCR on the same 2,376 scans. Word counts use normalized lexical tokens; dictionary matches use a pinned wiki100k word list. These differ from the 21.1 million whitespace-separated tokens in the raw output.

More words does not prove more correct text. Dictionary checks miss plausible substitutions and invented prose; names and historical spellings may fail them. Repetition, missing passages and reading-order errors remain. The original baseline is a page-mapped Archive OCR derivative—not verified PDF-embedded text.

How we got here

Columns → chunks → source-linked text

These figures show saved geometry from a real extraction. The blue line separates the masthead; green lines follow column gutters. Each smaller crop retains a polygon in the original scan. GLM’s text is linked to that crop, not aligned to individual words. Proposed boundaries can still cut through text.

  1. Tesseract baseline. Learned column templates, curved gutters and overlapping strips.
  2. Vision comparison. Tested DeepSeek, GLM and Paddle on identical inputs. Whole pages repeated; short column chunks worked better. GLM matched DeepSeek’s small pilot excerpt score: 0.52% character error.
  3. Spread correction. Stopped a flawed landscape run. Added separate leaves, printed-rule gutters and sloped mastheads.
  4. Faster extraction. Four concurrent requests, next-page preparation and decode CUDA graphs. Graphs delivered 3.43× the eager-mode benchmark throughput.
  5. Static publication. All 2,376 scans processed; 435 have output flags. Pagefind searches GLM text in your browser. Original OCR loads only when requested.
