01 The repair audit

Fewer loops. Fewer unfinished extractions.

The v5 edition reprocessed 6,445 suspect crops. We selected 4,189 replacements and retained 2,256 earlier readings with unresolved flags.

For this comparison, replacement pieces are joined back into the same 60,065 original source regions. Splitting a crop into smaller pieces cannot improve the denominator.

A health indicator is not an accuracy score. Replacements were screened for repetition and termination, so improvements on those measures are expected. They do not prove faithful transcription or complete page coverage.

The less obvious result

Dictionary coverage barely changed. Its missing-word share rose slightly even as loops disappeared: repeated common words had made the old output look healthier. Very long tokens also increased. Neither fluent prose nor a good dictionary score is enough to approve a reading.

Definitions, uncertainty & secondary checks

Repetition: count the union of redundant positions in eight-word phrases occurring at least four times. A high candidate has at least 80 redundant words and a 25% repeated footprint. Printed refrains and recurring notices can still trigger this diagnostic.

Dictionary misses: a pinned word list, Unicode normalization and dictionary-validated dehyphenation. Names and historical spellings may be absent. Parent-region percentages differ slightly from the homepage’s whole-page comparison because words can join across boundaries.

Whole-page check: mean scan-level repetition fell 1.519 percentage points. A 2,000-resample bootstrap over 44 source bundles gives a 95% interval of −1.728 to −1.327 points. Recurring notices are included.

Independent OCR comparison: mean bag-of-words disagreement with Archive OCR moved from 54.3623% to 54.2019%. This is agreement between two noisy systems, not a 54% error rate. Word order is ignored.

These intervals describe variation across source bundles, not uncertainty in unmeasured transcription accuracy. Download the aggregate measurements →

02 The evaluator experiment

More context didn’t make GLM a critic.

We gave GLM-OCR the same target with one, two and three nominal columns of extracted text. It saw no image, quality flag or reference judgment. The task was to identify a visible issue and quote evidence from the target.

The experiment covered 29 human-labeled crop layouts, 24 earlier scan-reviewed repairs and four synthetic controls. Those are 57 targets repeated across three context sizes, not 171 independent examples.

What it returned

Copied schema placeholders, continued newspaper text, or output that ran to the token limit. All inputs fit the context window.

Did simpler prompts help?

A yes/no prompt answered “yes” to every control. An empty-schema prompt returned ten usable answers—all “no visible issue.” Neither separated clean text from corruption.

Decision: keep GLM-OCR on recognition. This checkpoint did not work as a text-only quality judge under the tested prompts. Its documented scope is recognition and structured information extraction.

Model and experiment details

GLM-OCR revision 2e85a62840ccac27daa451df36c736c4636b8628; vLLM 0.28.0; BF16; temperature 0; 32,768-token context; 256-token response cap. The largest input was 14,668 tokens. No input was silently truncated.

The marked target was one extraction chunk inside complete nominal column groupings. Neighboring text stayed fixed. Geometry labels came from the earlier source-grouped test split. The 24 reviewed repairs predate v5 and do not estimate v5 accuracy.

The primary experiment made 171 calls. Two post-hoc prompts added 24 synthetic-control calls. Invalid responses were counted separately rather than credited as detections. No new evaluator was added to the production pipeline.

03 A useful measurement stack

Measure recognition, coverage and order.

  1. Generation health

    Track loops, token limits, request failures, empty output and extreme size changes. Cheap signals make good review queues.

  2. Source coverage & reading order

    Check image geometry and source-verified phrases. A missing column can leave behind completely plausible text.

  3. Transcription accuracy

    Measure character and word error rates against representative, verified transcriptions. Our small pilot references cannot establish corpus-wide accuracy.

  4. Detector usefulness

    Report caught failures, false alarms and abstentions separately. Compare new models with simple rules on source-held-out examples.
