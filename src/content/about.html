Evidence & limits

2,376 scans from 44 volumes of the AME Church’s newspaper. Penn’s catalog identifies the archived originals. This is an independent, uncorrected research edition.

Reading the numbers

Homepage statistics compare GLM with Archive’s existing OCR on the same scans. Counts use normalized word tokens and a pinned wiki100k list. Dictionary matches measure recognizable forms—not accuracy or recovered-page completeness.

Names can fail the dictionary; invented prose can pass it. Repetition, omissions and reading-order errors remain. Archive OCR is a page-mapped derivative, not verified PDF-embedded text. Download the measured comparison.

What the experiments established

  • Chunks beat whole pages. GLM and DeepSeek both reached 0.52% character error on two checked pilot excerpts; Paddle reached 0.78%, fixed-chunk Tesseract 5.88%.
  • Layout matters. A failed landscape run led to separate leaves, printed-rule gutters and sloped mastheads.
  • Graphs improved throughput. Four-request decode CUDA graphs were 3.43× faster than four-request eager execution on the same test workload.

Those references were provisional and previously inspected. They do not establish corpus-wide accuracy. Of the completed scans, 435 have output flags; others may still contain errors. Results, runtime & build details ↗
