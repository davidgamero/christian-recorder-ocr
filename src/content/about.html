A searchable research edition of The Christian Recorder, the African Methodist Episcopal Church’s newspaper. Machine transcriptions, always linked to the original scan.

01 The collection

2,376 scans. 44 archived volumes.

Penn’s serial catalog led us to the originals at Internet Archive. We extracted the small print into text you can search, browse and compare with the page.

Scan numbers include separator cards and two-page spreads; they are not printed newspaper page numbers. Year labels come from volume metadata, not verified issue dates.

Use it as a finding aid. Check the scan before quoting. Missing text, wrong names, repetition and plausible invented readings remain possible. This is an independent research edition, not an official Penn, Archive or AME publication.

02 From pixels to passages

Small crops preserve the tiny print.

Separate book spreads into leaves, locate printed gutters and mastheads, then recognize short regions with GLM-OCR. Four parallel requests and CUDA graphs keep the local GPU busy.

Real saved v4 geometry. Green marks proposed gutters, blue the masthead cutoff, red the OCR chunks. These are proposals, not verified boundaries or word-level coordinates.

Recognition

GLM and DeepSeek each reached 0.52% character error on two checked pilot excerpts; Paddle reached 0.78%, fixed-chunk Tesseract 5.88%.

Throughput

Four-request CUDA graphs ran 3.43× faster than four-request eager execution on the same 44-input benchmark.

Only 244 reference words informed those provisional pilot scores. They do not establish full-page recall or corpus-wide accuracy.

03 The current edition · v5

Repair the boundaries, then read again.

The targeted pass reprocessed 6,445 suspect regions across 1,496 scans. New crops use original pixels, tilted printed-rule cuts and bounded recognition. An early equal-width fallback was discarded after a preview showed it slicing through text.

Unresolved wide geometry or problematic new output keeps the prior text with a review flag. Original attempts remain preserved. Crop footprints stay fixed to avoid duplicating neighboring text, so already-clipped outer edges can still need correction.

04 Reading the numbers

Recognizable words aren’t necessarily correct words.

Homepage statistics compare GLM with Archive’s existing OCR on the same scans. Counts use normalized word tokens and a pinned dictionary. Archive OCR is a page-mapped derivative, not a verified PDF text layer.

Names can fail the dictionary; invented prose can pass it. Our repair study found much less repetition with almost no change in dictionary coverage. Accuracy, completeness and reading order need different checks.
